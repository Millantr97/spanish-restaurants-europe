London leads with 148 tracked Spanish restaurants, ahead of Lisbon (52) and Paris (50) - while Germany's spread is the real story: no single huge scene, but Spanish tables across dozens of mid-size cities. Per capita the picture flips: Porto leads with 14.2 Spanish venues per 100,000 residents - density, not size, is where the smaller cities win.

How we count: one listing is one venue, counted under the city Google reports for it. Because that field arrives messy, we strip postcodes, district and province codes (so "Roma RM", "London SE1 1TU" and "-007 Lisboa" all land on their city), ignore case and accents, and merge language variants of the same name (Milan = Milano, Munich = München, Cologne = Köln). The per 100k column then divides each city's venue count by its city-proper population - the density of Spanish tables per 100,000 inhabitants. Populations come from national statistics offices (compiled in Wikipedia's city population lists), checked 11 Sep 2026. Boundary note: London is counted as Greater London and Brussels as the Brussels-Capital Region, matching how our map pins cover the urban area; for the rest, city-proper boundaries apply, so where a statistical boundary is narrower than the area our listings cover, the rate is an approximation and we say so here rather than hide it.
